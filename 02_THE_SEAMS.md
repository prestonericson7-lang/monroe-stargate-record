# 02. The second pass: the seams

The second pass, 18 November 2026, run under a working frame chosen on purpose: act as if this were a cover-up, and leave no leaf unturned. The frame is a search strategy, not a conclusion. If the record hid something, the seams would show in the gaps, the contradictions, the money, the records referenced but absent, the redactions, and the later denials against the earlier files. So those are what was hunted, by name, in the CIA reading room's STARGATE and General CIA Records collections and in the two public mirrors that answered. Both readings are kept, as in `01`. Every document named here was read as section 5 states.

## 1. The seams, in the order of how much they change the picture

**S1. The first time the Army used Monroe's method in a live operation, the target was a kidnapped CIA station chief, and the picture the viewer gave was detailed, confident, and wrong by the public record.** The trip report of 28 March 1984 (`01`, document h) says the first operational Hemi-Sync interviews happened on 21 March 1984 and points to "Project 8404". Project 8404 is William F. Buckley, the CIA's Beirut station chief, kidnapped on 16 March 1984 (Summary Project 8404, 13 April 1984, CIA-RDP96-00788R001900530019-5, read whole: eight sessions by 11 April; "a minimum of twice weekly liaison with the CIA"; the first seven in present or past time, the eighth aimed at a future date, 20 April 1984, because "previous sessions seemed to indicate that he had been moved on at least three occasions"). The session itself is CENTER LANE Interview Summary HU-1070/8404/01 (CIA-RDP96-00788R001900550001-2, 14 pages). The reading room's text shows only the cover memo; the transcript, the agent report and the drawing exist only inside the PDF, which the reading room serves as a download. That PDF is in `documents/`, and all fourteen pages were read as images. It is the record the legend never quotes. Source #01 (McMoneagle, by the memos around it), monitor #66, 1:24 PM, 21 March 1984, the source "shown the photograph ... and asked to describe Buckley's location", "aware that Buckley ... was a kidnap victim", nothing else. The monitor reads skin voltage aloud through the session ("we're down 5 points on GSR"; "voltage reversal now"; at plus 49 minutes "voltage reversals back, that appears to be a memory data, not viewing data"), which is the "somatic electrical response" of the trip report, used live as a truth gauge. What the source gave: Buckley "not eating", "convinced that he's not going to make it", held "up to a certain point and then he's just going to be eliminated"; his captors "a joint operation of two groups", the smaller the stronger, with "Red Brigade" and "a quasi political group ... based in extreme Southern Europe" with "a political name ... less than three syllables ... starts with an F"; the place: a city on a river running north-northeast to south-southwest with a "Y" tributary, a cathedral with a dome, a cobblestone old quarter "up against the cliffs" on the east side, a "very cluttered, very modern" west side, "mountains circling", and a drawing of it (inclosure 3). The monitor at one point instructs: "Tell him that you know that he was a ranger in the army." The agent report (DA Form 341, headed PROJECT FRANCIS) ends: "Source was unable to provide any additional information concerning SUBJECT during this interview." Against the public record: Buckley was taken in Beirut, a coastal city with the sea to its west, held in Lebanon's Bekaa Valley, and, by the CIA's own open-source clipping of 12 December 1985 (CIA-RDP90-00965R000706950005-3, read whole; a Jack Anderson column), moved to Tehran and dead of a heart attack after months of torture; most public accounts put his death on or about 3 June 1985. No river, no cathedral, no southern Europe. The emotional read, a man who had given up and would be killed, matches what happened to him; the geography that was supposed to find him does not. The CIA's own evaluation of these sessions (the "sponsor") is not in what was found: gap G3 below.

**S2. The Army kept its own extent hidden from Monroe, not the other way around.** Trip report, 18-28 September 1980 (CIA-RDP96-00788R001200060028-4, read whole; Atwater, then a first lieutenant, took the Gateway course himself): "Monroe has not been 'read in' on IGFP and remains unaware of the extent of the program"; "All contact with MIAS has been on a 'shallow cover' basis"; "No other personnel at MIAS are aware of this agreement." The man in the legend as the government's insider was, by the Army's own memo, a cleared consultant kept on the outside. The same memo gives the 1980 training environment exactly: "surf sounds combined with a beat frequency oscillation of tones at four hertz", in a soundproof booth, fifty exercises in eight days.

**S3. A program Congress had defunded was continued under a new name with money found "out of hide".** The Army's own chronology, "List of Events from September 1977 to January 1986", 31 January 1986 (CIA-RDP96-00788R001100020001-8, 8 pages, read whole, released in part): 1 October 1982, "NFIP funding terminated for Army GRILL FLAME collection operations project by Congressional directive"; 3 December 1982, "CG INSCOM notifies ACSI DA that INSCOM will fund a psychoenergetics program out of hide by using S&IA funds, based on ACSI staff guidance. Project name changed to CENTER LANE"; 19 January 1983, the Under Secretary of Defense "signs memorandum allowing Program 6 resources to be used to maintain the current INSCOM CENTER LANE capability"; 15 June 1983, the Secretary of the Army approves a Special Access Program for it. The 1983 contract with Monroe was paid from "ICF" because "Use of ICF precludes the unauthorized disclosure of information classified SECRET/CL-4/NOFORN" (Advanced Individual Training for CENTER LANE Personnel, 19 July 1983, CIA-RDP96-00788R001700210007-5, read whole). This is the strongest hidden-from-oversight seam in the record, and it is the Army's own account of itself.

**S4. The Army's lawyers and doctors kept saying "human use", and the field kept going.** Same chronology: 15 February 1979, "Army General Counsel advised ACSI that GRILL FLAME constitutes human use"; the Surgeon General's review board "concludes GRILL FLAME not human use", then on 20 November 1979 "states Remote Viewing is human use"; 5 March 1980, Under Secretary of Defense Perry restricts P-6 funds: "Not appropriate for Army to fund technology programs aimed at scientific demonstration of parapsychology"; 17 July 1981, the General Counsel wants a charter submitted to the Secretary to comply with DoD 5240.1-R. The 1978 Pentagon paper (`01`, document c) had already cited the federal statute on experimentation on human beings. By 1984 the answer was informed-consent forms (`01`, document g).

**S5. The money, every number in the record.** $2,000 for the 1978 pilot, "four consecutive three day intense training sessions" (GONDOLA WISH memo, 18 May 1978, CIA-RDP96-00788R002000160007-7, read whole; MIAS "favorably recommended the selected trainee volunteers"). $24,400 from ICF for the 1983 one-on-one training of two personnel, Monroe providing custom tapes that "will become the property of this office" (the 19 July 1983 memo; the 26 August 1983 Executive Summary, CIA-RDP96-00788R001700220013-7, read whole: "Monroe has been paid 24,400.00 (ICF)"). RAPT at "$850.00 per person", "$60,000" for FY84 (the LTG Brown briefing, 7 March 1984, CIA-RDP96-00788R001500030001-3, 5 pages, read whole, released in part). Projected CENTER LANE spending on the chart shown to Stubblebine on 13 April 1984: FY84 $280K, FY85 $570K, FY86 $700K, with a line "Search-ELF/CM 50K" and a personnel plan growing from one operational source to six (Visit by CG, INSCOM, 16 April 1984, CIA-RDP96-00788R001700220008-3, read whole). Where the growth went instead: S7.

**S6. Two verdicts sixty days apart, and a first pilot that does not appear to have run.** The field office recommended Monroe (25 August 1978) and the Pentagon staff rejected him (27 October 1978) (`01`, documents b and c). In the chronology the first GRILL FLAME operative attends Gateway only in 1980, and by 20 November 1978 the Army's attention had moved to SRI and a psychokinesis switch (S8). No document was found saying the October-November 1978 out-of-body training happened. Gap G2.

**S7. The brass turned on it in writing, in the spring of 1984, and the record of that turn was shaped after the fact.** LTG Brown, Director of the Army Staff, 1 March 1984: "concerned any time the Army did things involving soldiers' minds"; "some might feel pressured to participate"; "fluttering" about an "in" group; asked whether TDY was paid "from a cash account" as in "a 'black program'", "his concern with the DCSOPS' investigation"; "appeared skeptical of INSCOM's involvement with MIAS training and did not appear convinced of the value"; MG Stubblebine "had asked the DAIG to review certain INSCOM programs to include CENTER LANE" (the 7 March 1984 memo, read whole). 13 March 1984: Brown "directs cancellation of RAPT as CENTER LANE personnel screening vehicle" (the chronology); a 16 April 1984 memo found by search but not read says the reason was "inadequate oversight of the INSCOM RAPT program with MIAS". 13 April 1984: Stubblebine "stressed that although RAPT had been cancelled the DAS had not objected to the use of Monroe Institute for training on a small scale. MG Stubblebine directed the preparation of an MFR on that point" (the 16 April 1984 memo, read whole): a memorandum for the record ordered to record a permission nobody else wrote down. The same memo: the CG wanted to shorten training "possibly through the use of NLP", floated Ingo Swann as a modeling subject (refused for "the OPSEC problem due to Swann's notoriety"), discussed "reading wives on to the project", and was "briefed in depth on a current on-going hi-level project (Buckley)". 13 July 1984, the CG decides to discontinue CENTER LANE; 23 August the Secretary approves; 28 September 1984 operations cease; 7 March 1985 operational control to DIA. Earlier, 31 March 1983, Vice President George Bush "was briefed for thirty minutes concerning CENTER LANE related activities by Dr. Vorona, DIA ... the Vice President was interested and impressed" (the chronology).

**S8. The force on a machine was an Army procurement item in 1978.** USofA GRILL FLAME Request, 20 November 1978 (CIA-RDP96-00788R002000010010-9, read whole): the Missile Intelligence Agency proposed four SRI tasks "that will lead to the development of an end item of equipment -- a remote switching device that can be triggered by mental processes (Psychokinesis)", because its officer "came away convinced that a major breakthrough has been achieved, whereby a human Subject successfully interfered mentally with a computer-driven random noise generator." That is the claim of test T2 in `04`, in the Army's own words, forty-seven years before it can be run sealed.

**S9. MKULTRA funded ESP research with hypnosis and drugs in 1961.** MKULTRA Subproject 136, 23 August 1961 (reading room document 00017395, 24 pages, read in the reading room's OCR, released in part, the investigator's name blanked): "The purpose of this subproject is to support the research of an 'Experimental Analysis of Extrasensory Perception'", $8,579 for one year, funded through a cut-out "for security and cover purposes"; the attached proposal says the investigators "are by no means certain that ESP exists", plans to map "channel capacity" and "the kinds of messages which can be transmitted", and "special attention will be given to dissociative states, which tend to accompany spontaneous ESP experiences. Such states can be induced and controlled to some extent with hypnosis and drugs." The Wellcome Collection catalogues papers of Stephen Abrams under the heading "MKULTRA subproject 136" (not read; the name is not asserted from the CIA's page, which blanks it). So the mind-control line and the ESP line meet in one 1961 memo, seventeen years before GONDOLA WISH.

**S10. The Institute, in its own bulletins kept in the Army's files, said it was not doing science and ran blind manipulations on paying participants.** M.I.A.S. Bulletin #22480G (CIA-RDP96-00788R001100200006-3, read whole): the method began "in the Research and Development Division of Monroe Industries, Inc., which at the time (1958) was investigating methods and techniques of accelerated learning"; it could "'program' sleep cycles throughout the night"; and: "The efforts of the Institute have not been designed or performed with the intent of offering documentation to the scientific community of the world at large." *The Explorer*, Vol. 1 No. 1, 1980 (CIA-RDP96-00788R001700210062-4, read whole): "The Monroe Institute has conducted Gateway training groups where, unknown to the participants, negative air-ion environment was present during some exercises in Focus 10 and absent during others"; a demonstration to "some 45 executives" at General Electric's Crotonville center with bilateral EEG on a dual-trace oscilloscope under Dr. Todd Mikuriya; "not one believed the demonstration time to be more than 15 minutes ... The actual length in real time was 45 minutes." Bulletin #PR31380H's "4,823 experiments among 1,280 volunteer participants" (`01`, document j) were, by the Institute's own words above, never meant for the scientific record.

**S11. The missing page 25 holds philosophy, and the CIA says it never had it.** The CIA's copy of the Gateway analysis goes from page 24 to 26. A paragraph-by-paragraph comparison against the copy the Monroe Institute put on archive.org in April 2021 (item "(TMI-Army Files) 1983 - Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process", creator given as McDonnell, read whole) finds exactly one difference: paragraphs 35 and 36, "Left Brain Limitations" and "Self Knowledge", page 25 (the text is in `documents/`). Their content: twentieth-century physics "revisiting insights" of the mystics; Gateway "a promising method for achieving the intuitive, holistic type of interface with the universal hologram"; "Know thyself"; "a portal through which, based on months if not years of practice, the individual may pass in his search to find self, personal effectuality, and truth in the larger sense." A researcher's 2020 FOIA letters (davisr.me, secondary, read) record INSCOM's answer: "They are unable to provide Page 25 of this document and do not have it in their collection." Both readings: a page pulled for a reason, or a page lost in the Army's copying in 1983 and kept by the author's own institute. The content is the same either way, and there is nothing operational on it.

**S12. McDonnell Douglas Astronautics is in the loop twice.** The Army's nineteen-page Hemisync report, 1 January 1984 (CIA-RDP96-00788R001700210004-8, read whole; author not named on it) cites "Houck, G. B. Entrainment Techniques. Unpublished research, McDonnell Douglas Astronautics Company, Huntington Beach, CA, 1984" for entraining brain waves through a standing wave set up in a water bed, and the chronology has "9 May 84 Dialogue initiated with McDonnell Douglas Astronautics Company, Huntington Beach, CA to comply with directive to obtain second contractor." The report itself is the Army's clearest statement of the mechanism it believed: entrainment by "amplitude modulated standing wave(s)" in each hemisphere's olivary nucleus, "Focus 10" as a delta cerebellum under a low-beta cortex, "as many as six individual binaural beat frequencies", remote viewing listed among the tasks the conventional beats serve, and its own conclusion that "Hemisync is the only method of learning to control both hemispheric specialization and/or interaction and brain wave frequencies." Its evidence for all of that is one unpublished raw-data citation (Edrington and Panagiotides 1984) and the Institute's bulletins.

**S13. Where the record is cut.** Redaction markers SG1A, SG1J and SGFOIA3 sit inside the Buckley summary, the Brown briefing, the Stubblebine visit memo and the chronology ("RIPPUB", released in part). The reading room's text body of HU-1070 shows only the cover memo; the transcript, the agent report and the drawing exist only inside the PDF, which the reading room serves as a download and the public mirrors did not serve at all. A reader who trusts the site's search text never sees what the viewer actually said.

## 3. The verdict, both ways

**In the frame, as instructed.** What the frame found that the plain pass did not: the program outlived its funding by a name change and hidden money; the Army's lawyers and doctors called it human use three times before anyone signed a consent form; the first operational use of Monroe's method was on a kidnapped CIA officer and produced a confident map of the wrong city while a skin-voltage meter was read as proof of contact; the Army hid its own extent from Monroe; the staff killed the training for lack of oversight and the project within months, while the field wrote memoranda to record permissions after the fact; the Vice President was briefed and impressed; the force on a machine was a procurement item in 1978; and the ESP line was inside MKULTRA with hypnosis and drugs in 1961. All of that is real, and all of it is written by the people who did it.

**Out of the frame.** What the frame did not find, anywhere in what was read: a document showing an ability that worked and was hidden. The missing page is philosophy. The one live test with a life on it failed. The Institute said in its own bulletin it was not producing documentation for science. The plainest reading of the whole record is this: a small group of believers inside the Army bought a method they could not measure, used it on the most important case they had, and were shut down by their own chain of command over oversight and human use, not over results, because results were never recorded in a way anyone could score. That leaves the question exactly where `01` left it, and the tests exactly where they were: T1 and T2 first, sealed (`04`).
