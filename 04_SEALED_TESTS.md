# 04. Sealed tests anyone can run, and a practitioner's ladder

## Why sealed, and why hardware

The record's failure is not that the believers were fools. It is that nobody sealed anything. The monitor in the Buckley session knew the case and fed lines; the 2 April viewer had seen the imagery; the Institute's 4,823 experiments were never written up; the DIA scored by customer feedback. A sealed test removes the experimenter from the loop at the three places the record shows leakage: the choice of the target, the exposure of the target, and the scoring.

A cheap board does this better than a person or a PC:

- **The target comes from hardware entropy**, a true random source on the board, health-tested, not from a person who might know the viewer.
- **Commit before, reveal after.** Before the session the board writes the target's hash (its SHA-256 fingerprint) to a log, and to anyone who wants a copy. After the session the report is hashed first; only then is the target opened. Neither can be changed afterward without breaking the fingerprints.
- **A hash chain.** Every record carries the hash of the one before it, so nothing can be inserted, removed or reordered later; a copy of the chain kept in a second place is the proof.
- **Scoring by a rule written before the first trial**, run by the board, with the exact probability of every partial score computed ahead.

Any microcontroller or FPGA board with a hardware random source and a hash function will do. The ones used for this project are two small FPGA boards that run the same program and print the same chain, so each result is measured twice on separate hardware. A PC can do all of it too; what a PC cannot do as easily is be trusted not to have been touched.

## Before any test: pre-registration

Every protocol below is run only after a short page states, in advance: the hypothesis, the number of trials, the scoring rule, what result would count as above chance, and what result would break the prediction. Filed predictions are kept beside their outcomes. A broken prediction is a finding, not a failure.

## The five protocols

**T1. The digits (the Army's own criterion).** The board draws ten decimal digits from its entropy, shows them on a screen in a closed room, and before the session writes their hash to the chain. After the session the viewer's report is hashed, and only then is the number opened. Ten digits read whole is a one-in-ten-billion event; the chance of every partial score (k digits right in place) is exact and pre-registered, so the Army's own loose claim that "most acquired enough of the digits" becomes a number. Cost: a screen and a day. This is the shape of Tart's 1968 experiment and of paragraph 33 of the Gateway analysis, done so that nobody can misremember.

**T2. The force on a machine (the 1978 "switch triggered by mental processes").** The board's true random source streams bits; a counter sums them in blocks (2^32 bits a block is convenient); intention and rest periods are assigned by a sealed schedule the operator cannot see until afterward; every block's count, its period and its hash go into the chain. The effect sizes the field reports are so small that a few hours of bits give a decisive test of them at five sigma, which no 1980s machine could do in a year. The size to test against is read from the 2006 meta-analysis before the block size is set, and the power calculation is part of the pre-registration. Nobody has to do anything but sit and intend, or not.

**T3. Hemi-Sync made exact.** The board generates the Army's own operational environments to the digit (a 100 Hz carrier with a 1.5 Hz binaural beat; a 200 Hz carrier with a 4 Hz beat; the 1980 training mix of surf with a 4 Hz beat) as phase-locked audio, and an EEG front-end at a few sites (tens of dollars) records the brain's electrical response through the board's analog input. A frequency-following response is either present at the beat frequency or it is not; the order of beat and no-beat sessions is sealed; one subject, many sessions. This is the measurement the 2023 systematic review says the literature never settled, done on the one person who wants to know and can sit still for it.

**T4. The somatic correlate.** Atwater's claim with a number on it: a sealed-target description task with skin potential recorded through the same input, targets hash-committed, scoring by a rule written before the first trial. If the electrical response tracks accuracy it will show as a pre-registered correlation; if not, that claim is measured too. The Army used this meter live as a truth gauge in the Buckley session; nobody ever calibrated it.

**T5. The future number.** The report is hashed first, and the number is generated by the board afterward, from its entropy. The only way to score is the way the DIA said was "simply not marketable". It costs nothing beyond T1 and settles the one claim the program itself conceded.

The first build is T2 and T1 together: both need only the board, a screen and a sealing script, and neither needs the person to do anything but sit. T3 and T4 wait on the EEG front-end.

## A practitioner's ladder, taken from the DIA's own training classes

The 1988 SUN STREAK report describes three training classes for coordinate remote viewing (`03`, 1b): Class C, immediate positive-only feedback from a monitor who holds the answer; Class B, the monitor knows the site but gives no feedback and may only ask for elaboration of what the viewer already said; Class A, the monitor knows nothing and the viewer gets nothing. It also describes a program, "Abstract Referents Discrimination of Binary Alternatives", which opens by admitting that "Remote Viewers have demonstrated little ability to discriminate alphanumeric information" and then trains exactly that. The ladder below is that progression with the board as the monitor that never leaks. It is written for someone who already has a practice; a beginner starts at step 1 with whatever they can do.

1. **The baseline on a meter.** Whatever the practitioner already does, recorded as it is: EEG at a few sites, an eye channel, skin voltage, breath; the stage reached, in the practitioner's own name for it; and the practitioner's estimate of elapsed time against the clock. A week of this is the first measured description of the practice. The Institute's own definition of its state, EEG "simultaneously equal in amplitude and frequency" in both hemispheres, meets the trace in week one. Filed prediction: the state shows a measurable change from the waking baseline in coherence or dominant frequency within the first week; broken if the traces are indistinguishable from eyes-closed rest.
2. **The loop.** Live feedback of coherence and dominant band, as a tone or a bar: the biofeedback road Monroe's own laboratory used and the Army's 1978 report describes. The tones of T3 are available as a ramp if wanted and absent if not; when they are used, the sealed schedule applies, so the test learns whether the tones add anything to a practitioner who no longer needs them.
3. **The ladder, Class C to A, with the board as the blind monitor.** Targets from a sealed pool drawn by the board's entropy, the hash committed before the session, the score by a rule written first, the answer opened only then. Class C (positive feedback only, from the board, no human in the loop), then B, then A. The first exercise is the DIA's own binary discrimination: two alternatives, hundreds of trials, exact statistics, the thing the program said viewers were worst at, which is why it is the cleanest. Filed prediction: binary discrimination at chance over the first 1,000 sealed trials; broken by a run the chain says is five sigma out, replicated the next day.
4. **The sealed tests from inside the state.** T1, T5, T2 and T4 run from the practised state, with the meter recording the state so that it is on the record while the number is sealed.
5. **The out-of-body line.** If the practitioner has spontaneous separations, the "vibration state", exits, then the Army's "discrete state" and "First Stage Separation" are the state to run T1 from, with the eye channel and the EEG recording the moment. If not, the published, trainable twin of the experience is lucid dreaming, verified in sleep laboratories by eye signals made from inside REM; the sealed digits test can be run from there.
6. **What a result does, either way.** Above chance anywhere: a second person, independent judging, replication, then a paper with the chain as the proof. At chance everywhere: the practice is measured for the first time, it has a feedback loop it never had, and the record holds the first sealed tests of this method ever run.

## The rules of the road (what the record itself teaches)

- **No one leads a person in the state.** The 1978 Pentagon paper's objection was "some sort of interrogation by Monroe or an assistant" in a suggestible state, and the Buckley transcript shows the monitor feeding lines ("tell him that you know that he was a ranger"). Sessions are solo, with any prompts recorded in daylight and played by the board; nobody speaks into a session.
- **Measured sessions are sober.** The Institute's own bulletin lists drug-induced states among those that do not produce the state it sells, and the 1961 MKULTRA proposal is the record of what hypnosis and drugs in dissociative states were for.
- **The Institute's own caution, kept.** Its 1983 analyst wrote that forcing the "patterning" exercises risks "dislocating his existing reality with drastic consequences" (paragraph 30B). If a session leaves a person adrift past an hour, or sleep worsens for a week, that phase stops and the stop is written down. Someone close to the practitioner knows the plan.
- **Grounding before depth.** The states in this record are absorption states, and absorption is the opposite of being present. Anyone with a history of dissociation or trauma should talk to a clinician who knows those words before practising; "here" is trained first, on the same meter, and the deep states wait. This is not medical advice; it is the one safety rule the record itself most clearly teaches.
- **No belief required and none refused.** Every phase pays off in a number whether the far end is there or not.

## What it costs

Nothing for the audio and the sealing scripts; the EEG front-end and electrodes for T3, T4 and the ladder; an afternoon of board time for T2; and the practitioner's mornings for a few weeks.
